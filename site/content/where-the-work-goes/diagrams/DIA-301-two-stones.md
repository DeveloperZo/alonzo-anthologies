# DIA-301: Two Stones
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/where-the-work-goes/diagrams/DIA-301-two-stones.svg`
- **Theme:** The two extremes a delivery process can fall into once the work moves faster than it can be checked
- **Primary audience takeaway:** A stone too fast to sweep and a stone too slow to arrive score the same. The sweeper holding the broom can help neither.

## What The Diagram Shows
An ink-brush scene on a frozen pond, under two washes of far hills. A robed figure stands in the middle of the ice with a broom held upright. To the right, a curling stone has already gone past, trailing dry streaks, and is heading for the reeds at the far end. Far to the left, beside a small figure who threw it, a second stone sits where it stopped, well short.

The picture depicts the teaching in the essay's curling passage. It adds no argument of its own and carries no labels.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that going too fast and not going at all end in the same place, and two stones on one sheet of ice show it because the sweeper stands between them and can reach neither.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** the ice (one long dry stroke), the thrower (small, far left), the stone that stopped short, the sweeper with a broom (centre, the largest figure), the stone that ran past (right, with streaks behind it), reeds at the far end, two hill washes, and a seal in the lower right corner.
- **Relationships:** read left to right, the direction the stones travel. The slow stone never reaches the sweeper. The fast stone is already beyond the sweeper.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** none. Rust is used for the two stone handles and the seal.

## How To Read It
1. Find the sweeper in the middle, standing still with the broom.
2. Look right: a stone has passed and cannot be swept now.
3. Look left: a stone has stopped and sweeping will not move it.
4. Read the caption: on the scoreboard the two are the same.

## Delivery Relevance
The essay's curling passage says that AI does not replace the player who aims or the teammates who sweep. It makes the stone faster. A team that lets everything through has built an unstoppable force, and a team that checks everything until nothing ships has built an immovable object. Curling's rules remove both kinds of stone from play (source 13), so the comparison is literal. This picture is that passage, drawn.

Drawing tradition: ink-brush painting, used the way a teaching fable is illustrated: a few strokes, a lot of empty paper, one small scene. It is not in the engraved plate style of the governance series and has no frame or script title. This is the fifth form tried for this essay's diagram. Three engraved object plates, an equal-area bar chart, a hand-drawn comic and an engraved heron came before it, on 2026-11-10 and 11.

How the brush look is made: three SVG filters. One wanders the outline of filled shapes (a wet edge), one cuts bristle gaps along a stroke (a dry brush), and one blurs a pale fill (a wash). The seal's mark is a plain ring, not writing.

What it leaves out: the captain, the target, the second sweeper, and anything about software. The essay's prose makes the connection.
