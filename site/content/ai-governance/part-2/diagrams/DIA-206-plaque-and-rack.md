## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of the wall inside a works entrance. A screwed-on plaque reads FOR THE BENEFIT OF ALL. Below it stand a time clock and a card rack of nine pockets. Five hold a card. Four are empty, in rust, and the four pulled cards lie in a wire bin on the floor.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that a system's purpose is read from what it does and not from what it says, and a works wall shows it because the plaque gives the words while the card rack beneath it keeps the count.

Tradition: office-equipment catalogue plates.

## How To Read It
1. Read the plaque.
2. Count the cards in the rack, then the gaps.
3. Look down to the bin.

## Leverage Relevance
The plate sits under tier 4's opening paragraph and above "What the System Is Actually For", whose line reads "not the mission statement, but what deployment patterns consistently produce". The author asked for a visceral depiction of actions against words.

What it leaves out: assumptions and coordination, the other two points of the tier. Why the cards were pulled. The plaque wording is generic and quotes no company. The figure is an executive's own statement about what AI did.
