# DIA-201: A Load Line, Repainted
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-2/diagrams/DIA-201-drift-to-low-performance.svg`
- **Theme:** Drift to low performance in a self-imposed standard
- **Primary audience takeaway:** A standard set by the party it binds moves to match what that party is already doing.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of a riveted ship's hull seen from the side, in the tradition of naval architecture plates. Cargo is stacked on deck and one more crate is being lowered. Four load-line marks are painted on the hull in a rising column. The lowest is far under water, struck through, and noted `autumn 2023, first painted: pause if capability outruns control`. Two more above it are also submerged and struck through. The newest, in rust, sits just above the waterline.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that a self-set safety standard drifts toward actual behavior, and a load line shows it because an owner who paints their own mark can always paint it above the water.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - The hull, the waterline, and the water drawn over the submerged plates.
  - Four marks: the original and three repaintings.
  - Deck cargo, and one crate coming aboard, noted `cargo taken on to keep pace`.
  - Legend line: `The Merchant Shipping Act of 1876 made the mark compulsory, and left its height to the owner.`
- **Relationships:**
  - More cargo sits the ship lower. Each repainting puts the mark back above the water.
  - Older marks stay visible under the surface, so the history is readable.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - `autumn 2023`: first version of Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy, with the pause commitment.
  - `February 2026, the fourth revision: no pause`: version 3.0 of 24 February 2026. Versions 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 came before it and kept the pause.
  - `Four fresh coats since, same height`: versions 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4, through 8 July 2026. None restored the pause. Source: Anthropic's policy updates page, and Part 1 source 13e.
  - The plate draws four marks for five versions up to February 2026. The two middle marks stand for the three revisions that kept the pause.
  - The two middle marks are unlabeled on purpose. The text does not say what each revision changed.

## How To Read It
1. Find the waterline and the rust mark just above it.
2. Look down through the water to the three struck marks.
3. Read the note beside the lowest one.
4. Look up at the deck and the crate being lowered.

## Leverage Relevance
Part 2 opens on the reader's sense that pledges do not hold. The plate shows why without accusing anyone of lying: each mark was a true statement about the ship on the day it was painted. The standard has no anchor outside the actor. The historical line under the title points at the remedy the rest of Part 2 builds toward, which is a mark someone else sets.

History note: this plate draws the drift idea from the original spec comment in `00-why-you-feel-helpless.md`. The earlier SVG under this file name was a say-versus-do table. The 1876 statement in the legend was checked on 2026-11-10 against the Lloyd's Register Foundation heritage page on the Plimsoll line: the 1876 Act left owners to decide where the line was painted, and its position was fixed by law in 1890.
