"In January 2025, Sam Altman pledged to crack down on rivals distilling OpenAI's outputs in the same week his lawyers were arguing in court that training on copyrighted books was fair use no AI lab could survive without."

## I. Source-bias read-through

Disclosure, as before: this read was done by an Anthropic model, and Anthropic is a subject of the piece.

**I1. Part 2 does not flatter the labs.** Its sources are court filings, layoff notices and the companies' own announcements read against them. Anthropic appears twice, both times unfavourably (the dropped pause, the $1.5 billion settlement).

**I2. Two passages extend the labs a courtesy.** Section 0: "The pressure Kaplan names is real. The lab is not lying about it." This is the pattern decided as "leave" in the Part 1 file (I6). Left. Tier 4 and the quick reference recommend funding interpretability research as the route to legible risk. Most of that research is done inside the labs, and Part 1 section 3 records that it explains about a quarter of the prompts its authors tried. Option: add "independent" before "interpretability research".

**I3. The lean runs the other way, and four claims outrun their sources.** These are A1 (words put in Altman's mouth), A2 (AI named in a memo that did not name it), A3 (remarks relocated to earnings calls) and E1 (intent). Each makes the companies look worse than the record does. A hostile reader would start with them.

**I4. Company claims taken at face value.** Benioff's 9,000 to 5,000 and Nadella's 30% are executives' own statements about what AI did, and both had reasons to overstate. Part 1 section 6 now attributes the 30% figure to the chief executive. Section 0 states it as what "Microsoft tells".
