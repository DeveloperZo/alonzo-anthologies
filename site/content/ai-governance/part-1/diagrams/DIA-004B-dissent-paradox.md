# DIA-004B: The Shortest Stave
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-1/diagrams/DIA-004B-dissent-paradox.svg`
- **Theme:** The dissent paradox: a safety standard tied to the lowest bar any competitor will hold
- **Primary audience takeaway:** One lab's higher safety commitment does not raise the standard. The standard sits at the lowest commitment in the field, so the higher one gets cut down to match.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of an open wooden tub, built of staves of unequal height and bound by two iron hoops, in the tradition of the stave-barrel figure used to teach the law of the minimum (often called Liebig's barrel; that attribution is from memory and unverified). Water stands inside exactly level with the top of the shortest stave, spills over it, runs down the outside and pools on the floor. One stave is drawn as a dashed rust outline rising far above the others: where Anthropic's binding pause commitment stood. The stave beneath it now ends in a fresh rust saw cut at about the height of its neighbors, and the sawn-off length lies on the floor beside the tub.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that a safety commitment higher than a competitor's does not raise the standard and so gets cut down, and a stave tub shows it because water stands only as high as the shortest stave, whatever the height of the others.

Three mappings, no lettered legend:
- a stave is one lab's safety commitment
- the water line is the standard in practice
- the shortest stave is the lowest bar any competitor is willing to hold

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - The tub: sixteen staves, eight seen from outside and eight from inside, two hoops. Stave heights other than the shortest and the cut one are illustrative and stand for no named lab.
  - The water surface, level with the shortest stave, and the spill over it.
  - The ghost stave (dashed rust), the saw cut (rust), and the offcut on the floor (rust). The offcut is drawn the same length as the ghost, 150 units.
- **Relationships:**
  - Water height is set by the shortest stave alone.
  - The tall stave held no more water while it stood, which is the reason given for cutting it.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - Epigraph: "We felt that it wouldn't actually help anyone for us to stop training AI models." Jared Kaplan of Anthropic, to TIME. Source: section 4, the callout "The safety policy that weakened itself in public" (reference 13).
  - "The lowest bar any competitor is willing to hold": the wording of the same callout.
  - "Sawn off 24 February 2026": the date the Responsible Scaling Policy update removed the binding pause commitment (same callout).
  - "Four revisions since. Not put back.": section 4 says four further revisions through July 2026 adjusted thresholds and reporting, and none restored the binding pause (reference 13e).

## How To Read It
1. Find the water line and follow it to the notch where it spills: the shortest stave.
2. Look at the other staves. All stand above the water and none of them holds any.
3. Read the dashed rust stave: a commitment that stood much taller and held no more water.
4. Find its sawn-off length on the floor, with the date.

## Governance Relevance
Section 4 names three weaknesses in the stabilizing loop: under-resourced oversight, unresolved liability, and the dissent paradox. DIA-004A covers the first with money at true scale. This plate covers the third and carries no money at all. It shows why a voluntary commitment by one lab cannot do the work of a rule that binds every lab: only raising the shortest stave raises the water.

What the plate leaves out, compared with the diagram it replaces ("Misaligned forces, emptied response"): the three forward forces (\$300B+ revenue a year, 100,000+ AI jobs, \$1.4T committed), the three braking forces (\$0 enforced US federal AI law, antitrust, the 5 to 7 year market verification lag), and the \$1.4T against \$400K bar with its 3.5 million to one ratio. The money comparison is already made by DIA-004A. The force figures do not appear in the section 4 prose. The earlier version of this spec described a third reading (safety dissenters leaving to found new labs, with SSI and Thinking Machines as examples); that is not in the section 4 prose either and is not drawn. The liability cases and the 156 enforcement actions from the neighboring callout are also left to the prose.
