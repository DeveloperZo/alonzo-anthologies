# DIA-004C: The Heavier Ball Keeps Going
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-1/diagrams/DIA-004C-momentum.svg`
- **Theme:** Momentum. Spending against oversight, drawn as two weights that are about to meet
- **Primary audience takeaway:** The money behind deployment outweighs the largest oversight budget by about ten thousand to one, and when two swinging weights meet, the heavier one keeps going.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of two wrecking balls hung from one riveted girder, swinging toward each other and about to touch. The left ball fills the plate and hangs on a heavy chain. The right ball is the size of a marble on a wire, and is the only rust element. Both are the same iron, so their weights are in proportion to their volumes.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that oversight money cannot check deployment money, and two wrecking balls show it because when two swinging masses collide the heavier one barely slows.

Tradition: mechanics-primer plates of pendulums and colliding spheres.

Working plate: `_KNOWLEDGE/Diagrams/proposals/PLATE-004C-wrecking-balls.svg`, generated by `PLATE-004C-wrecking-balls.gen.mjs`. Every figure is in one block at the top of the generator.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - The large ball: OpenAI's projected compute spending through 2030, `$856 billion`. Radius 160 units.
  - The small ball, in rust: the UK AI Security Institute's budget for one year, `$84 million`. Radius 7.4 units.
  - The girder, chains, motion marks and ground line. These carry no meaning.
- **Relationships:**
  - Scale: weight is drawn true. `$856B / $84M = 10,190`. For two balls of one material the radius goes with the cube root of the weight, so the small radius is `160 / cbrt(10,190) = 7.4`. The small ball is about a twenty-second as wide.
  - The plate has two mappings and no legend.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - `$856 billion`: section 2, source 6b. A leaked internal projection, not a company statement. The same figure is on DIA-002A and DIA-004A.
  - `$84 million`: carried over from DIA-004A, where it is in turn carried over from the earlier bar chart. It has no entry in `sources.md`.
  - `US CAISI's ball, at $10 million a year, would be half as wide`: source 26a. `cbrt(10 / 84) = 0.49`.
  - `If they meet at equal speed, the large ball keeps all but a five-thousandth of its own`: physics of the drawn object, for a head-on collision at equal and opposite speeds in which the balls lock together. The large ball keeps `(M - m) / (M + m)` of its speed, so it loses `2m / (M + m) = 1/5,096`.

## How To Read It
1. Look at the large ball and read its label.
2. Find the small one beside it.
3. Read the note on what happens when they meet.

## Governance Relevance
Section 2 says "You cannot stop an eighteen-wheeler with the brakes of a sedan." Section 4 says a brake that engages only after the vehicle is moving is not a licensing regime. This plate draws the first sentence with the section 4 figures. It sits beside DIA-004A, which shows the same ratio as widths on one scale. DIA-004A answers "how small is the oversight money". This plate answers "what happens when the two meet".

What it leaves out, and where it could mislead:
- The large figure is a multi-year projection and the small one is a single year's budget. Five years of the UK budget at today's rate would make the small ball about 1.7 times as wide. DIA-004A has the same mismatch and says so.
- Dollars are not momentum. The plate uses weight as a picture of scale, and makes no claim about speed.
- Only one oversight body is drawn. The largest AI fine to date and CAISI are on DIA-004A.
