# DIA-003: The Runout
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-1/diagrams/DIA-003-capability-gap.svg`
- **Theme:** The distance between what models can do and what can be ensured
- **Primary audience takeaway:** The gap does not predict a failure. It sets what a failure would cost, and it grows with every step taken above the last point of control.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of a rock face in section, in the tradition of survey and mountaineering plates. The rock interior is drawn as seven beds with survey-section textures (stipple, broken dashes, cross-bedding) and jointing; the textures are decoration and carry no data. A lead climber, drawn as a filled silhouette in hat, jacket, knapsack and boots, is high on the face, just above a level marked `FRONTIER`. Ledges already passed are marked `GPT-3, 2020`, `ChatGPT, 2022` and `GPT-4, 2023`. The rope runs down from the climber's waist to four anchors, all close to the ground, the highest just above the level marked `ROUTINE WORK`, and then out to a belayer, a second silhouette standing braced on the ground with the rope in both hands and the slack coiled behind. A rust dimension line marks the rope between the last anchor and the climber and is labeled `the runout`. The face is torn off at the top of the plate because it keeps going.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that capability has climbed far past what can be ensured, and a lead climb shows it because the length of rope above the last anchor decides how far a slip falls.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - The lead climber, with the note `The lead: what the newest models can do.`
  - The anchors, with the note `Anchors: what we can ensure.`
  - The belayer, with the note `The belay: whoever has to catch it.`
  - There are no key letters. Each note sits beside its part.
  - Four dotted levels across the plate: `ROUTINE WORK`, `EXPERT`, `TOP HUMAN`, `FRONTIER`.
  - Three dated ledges on the face.
  - The runout, dimensioned in rust.
- **Relationships:**
  - Height on the face is capability. The anchors are control, and they stop low.
  - The rope is continuous from belayer to climber, so the two are one system.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - There is no measured data. The line under the title says so: `Heights are judged, not measured.`
  - Ledge dates are release years: GPT-3 2020, ChatGPT 2022, GPT-4 2023.

## How To Read It
1. Find the climber and read the level beside them.
2. Follow the rope down until it meets an anchor.
3. Read the rust dimension between the two.
4. Look at where the anchors sit against the four levels.

## Governance Relevance
Section 3 says alignment is unsolved and does not close on its own. The plate avoids ambient dread: nothing is falling. It shows the mechanism instead. Exposure is set by the unprotected distance, and that distance grows whenever capability moves and control does not.

What it leaves out: any time axis for the anchors, and the RLHF mechanism the section goes on to explain.
