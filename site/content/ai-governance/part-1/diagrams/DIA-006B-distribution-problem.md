# DIA-006B: The Far Bank
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-1/diagrams/DIA-006B-distribution-problem.svg`
- **Theme:** Timing mismatch in labor transition
- **Primary audience takeaway:** More jobs are projected on the far side than are lost on the near side, but the last two crossings took decades, and nothing spans the gap.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of a gorge in section, in the tradition of a surveyor's section drawing. A short stone pier stands on the near bank: 92 million jobs displaced. A taller pier stands on the far bank: 170 million created. Dashed lines show where a span would run. None is drawn in ink. The width of the gorge is drawn to a scale of years. Two rust measuring lines cross it: 20 to 30 years for the internet transition, and 40 years, the full width, for the first industrial revolution. A note at the near edge names who stands closest to it.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that a positive job total does not help the people displaced, because the new work arrives decades later, and a gorge with a taller pier on the far bank shows it because what is built over there is no use to anyone on this side until something crosses the gap.

The working plate is `_KNOWLEDGE/Diagrams/proposals/PLATE-006B-far-bank.svg`.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - Near pier, `92 million displaced`, and far pier, `170 million created`. Both from the WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025, projected to 2030, as cited in section 6.
  - The gap, with two measures in rust: `20 to 30 years`, the internet transition (solid to 20, dashed to 30), and `40 years`, the first industrial revolution, when UK productivity grew and wages stagnated. Both from section 6.
  - Near-edge note: 6.1 million US workers, 4.2% of the workforce, clerical and administrative, 86% women, in smaller metro areas. NBER, January 2026, as cited in section 6.
  - Soft note in the gap: how wide it is for AI is contested. Section 6 says AI's pace may outrun the earlier window and that how much faster is contested.
- **Relationships:**
  - Three mappings: near pier is jobs displaced, far pier is jobs created, the width of the gap is years.
  - Rust marks the two measures of the gap and nothing else.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - Scale of years: 14 units to the year, so the gorge is 560 units wide for 40 years, with ticks every 10 years.
  - Scale of the piers: 1.5 units to the million. One course of stone is 15 units, or 10 million jobs. The near pier is 138 units high and the far pier 255.
  - The 6.1 million are a US figure from a different source than the global WEF totals, so they are a note, not a drawn quantity.
  - Left out, compared with the earlier diagram: the IMF cascade (60% of advanced-economy employment exposed, split about half augmented and half at substitution risk). The earlier drawing ran that cascade down into the 6.1 million as if one were derived from the other. They come from different studies, and the prose carries the IMF figures. The earlier diagram dated the IMF figure April 2025. Section 6 says January 2024. Also left out: "limited local alternatives" as a separate tag, the quotation "Not a career setback. Closer to permanent." (it does not appear in the section), and the closing line about the wrong unit of measurement.

## How To Read It
1. Compare the two piers. The far one is taller.
2. Look at what lies between them, and at the dashed line where a span would be.
3. Read the two rust measures across the gap, and the scale of years under the longer one.
4. Read the note at the near edge.

## Governance Relevance
Section 6 takes the net-positive projection seriously and locates the problem in distribution: new jobs do not align with displaced workers by skill, geography or wage level, and earlier transitions took 20 to 40 years to pay out. Policy that reads only the two totals sees a surplus. The plate puts the delay, which is where the harm falls, in the middle of the picture.
