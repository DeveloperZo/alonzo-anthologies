## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate set as a printed table, in the tradition of the letterpress tables in nineteenth-century handbooks. No object was drawn as a metaphor: the content is four rows of words, so it is typeset, with a graduated rule as the only artwork. The rule runs down the left edge with twelve marks, `12` at the top and `1` at the bottom, one per leverage point. Brackets group the marks in threes into four tiers. Each tier name is set larger than the one above it (19, 23, 27, 31 units), and the last is in rust. Beside each tier the table gives what a push there does and one precedent.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that the twelve points fall into four tiers and that a push does more the further down the scale it lands, and a numbered rule with a table beside it shows it because the tiers are positions on one ordered scale, the same twelve marks the next plate (DIA-008B) turns into a lever arm.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:**
  - The rule: twelve marks at even spacing, no subdivisions, because the ranking is ordinal.
  - Four brackets, three marks each: `12` to `10`, `9` to `7`, `6` to `4`, `3` to `1`.
  - Column `TIER`: the tier name and what it contains.
    - Low: parameters, buffers, physical structure
    - Medium: delays, feedback strength, loop gain
    - High: information flows, rules, who writes them
    - Transformative: goals, paradigm, coordination
  - Column `A PUSH HERE`: one sentence per tier, from the opening paragraph of section 8 (low, high, transformative) and from the earlier diagram (medium).
  - Column `PRECEDENT`:
    - Low: a tell, not a precedent. Most lab safety announcements live here, and the tell is no change in who is liable or who audits.
    - Medium: ASRS, since 1976. Voluntary near-miss reporting in aviation with limited immunity. No AI equivalent exists.
    - High: Twin Rivers. Households shown their daily electricity use cut it about 10% in a month.
    - Transformative: Denmark's flexicurity (the goal moved from stable jobs to workers who stay viable through transitions) and the Montreal Protocol (scientists made the threat legible and gave actors a graceful way out).
- **Relationships:**
  - Position down the rule is rank. Type size grows with it.
  - The accent is on the transformative tier only: its name and its bracket.
- **Notable labels or metrics:**
  - `1976`: ASRS established under a NASA and FAA agreement. Source note 38.
  - `about 10%`: Twin Rivers, about 10.5% over one month. Source note 39.
  - "Voluntary" follows source note 38 and the earlier diagram. The quick reference page (`leverage-points.md`, point 9) calls ASRS "mandatory, anonymous". The plate does not follow that wording.

What the plate leaves out, compared with the card diagram it replaces: the full diagnostic tell (deployment gates), the product liability ruling example, the 4 to 12% meta-analysis range, Denmark's 5% of GDP figure, the point numbers attached to each precedent (9, 5, 3, 1), the AI-equivalent line under each tier, and the note that windows narrow. Section 8 is a teaser. Part 2 and the source notes carry those.

## How To Read It
1. Read down the rule from `12` to `1`.
2. Read the four tier names, each larger than the last.
3. For each tier, read across: what a push there does, then where it has been done.
4. End on the rust tier at the bottom.
