# DIA-005: A Governor, Unpinned
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `site/content/ai-governance/part-1/diagrams/DIA-005-loops.svg`
- **Theme:** A balancing loop that senses but does not act, and an alarm wired to the accelerator
- **Primary audience takeaway:** The system senses its own speed and sounds the alarm. Nothing connects the alarm to the throttle, and the alarm is answered with more fuel.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate, in the tradition of nineteenth-century engineering plates, cropped to one mechanism. A large flyball governor stands over a steam pipe. A belt from the flywheel, which runs off the corner of the plate, spins it, and its two heavy balls are flung wide. A long lever passes through the governor's raised sleeve. Its left end points at the throttle valve on the pipe and stops at an empty eye. The rod that should join it to the throttle arm is drawn only as a rust dashed line, and the real rod lies on the floor beside its pin. The lever's right end pushes a hammer against a bell on the wall. By the firebox there is a heap of coal and a shovel.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that oversight detects harm and still does not slow the system, and a governor shows it because a governor only regulates an engine through the rod that connects it to the throttle.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** each is labeled by a short note beside it, read left to right as one sentence.
  - The governor: `oversight: it reads the speed correctly`.
  - The belt and flywheel: `capability, read as speed`.
  - The missing rod, in rust: `the rod to the throttle was never fitted`. The real one is noted `the rod, still on the floor`.
  - The throttle valve: `throttle: wide open`.
  - The bell: `so the alarm rings`.
  - The coal and shovel: `and is answered with coal`.
- **Relationships:**
  - The belt carries flywheel speed to the governor. The balls rise and lift the sleeve.
  - The sleeve tips the lever. One end should close the throttle and cannot. The other end rings the bell.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** none. The plate is structural.

## Which loops it draws
The working labels from the earlier stock-and-flow version still apply.

- **L4, the weak brake** (harm, oversight, investment): the governor and its missing rod.
- **L3, the inverted loop** (harm raises spending): the bell and the coal.
- **L1 and L2 are not drawn.** The forming loop where capability feeds its own training, and the capability-gap investment loop, are carried by the prose of section 5.

## How To Read It
1. Start at the governor. It is spinning and its balls are out.
2. Follow the lever left to the rust gap, then down to the rod on the floor.
3. Follow the lever right to the bell.
4. Finish at the coal by the firebox.

## Governance Relevance
The series title asks whether AI can self-govern. A governor is the original self-governing device, and it works only when its sensing is mechanically tied to the thing it limits. The plate separates two failures that section 5 describes: the brake that does not bind, and the alarm that raises investment.

History: an earlier version of this plate drew the whole engine small, with seven lettered keys. It was rejected as not up to quality. This version crops to the governor and replaces the keys with notes.
