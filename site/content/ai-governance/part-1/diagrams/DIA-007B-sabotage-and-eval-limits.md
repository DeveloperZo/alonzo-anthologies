# DIA-007B: The Needle and the Works
## Snapshot

- **Theme:** How far a model's written reasoning can be used to check what it did
- **Primary audience takeaway:** A gauge reports only through the link to its works. In most of the cases that matter, the model's written reasoning is not linked to what it did.

## What The Diagram Shows
An engraved plate of one large pressure gauge standing on a pipe. Its needle rests near the low end of the scale, beside the words `all well`. Part of the face is broken away. Through the break the curved pressure tube and a toothed sector can be seen. The link that should join the tube to the sector hangs loose from the tube. Where it should run is drawn as a rust dashed line to an empty rust eye.

Claim sentence: the reader should see that a model's written reasoning cannot be relied on as a reading of what it did, and a gauge shows it because a needle reports pressure only through the link that joins it to the works.

Tradition: instrument-maker's cutaway plates.

Working plate: `_KNOWLEDGE/Diagrams/proposals/PLATE-007B-gauge.svg`, generated by `PLATE-007B-gauge.gen.mjs`. The earlier counting-frames plate is still in `proposals/` as `PLATE-007B-counting-frames.svg`. The author found it uninteresting (2026-11-10).

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** three mappings, each given by a note beside the part.
  - The dial and needle: `the model's written reasoning, the only part an evaluator can read`.
  - The works behind the face: `what the model did`.
  - The missing link, in rust: `the link between them is off`.
- **Relationships:**
  - The gauge is mechanically right for a Bourdon gauge: a curved tube fixed at the stem, a link from its free tip to a toothed sector, and the sector turning a pinion on the needle spindle. With the link off, the needle cannot move whatever the tube does.
  - The scale has no numbers, only `all well` and `alarm`, so the needle position cannot be read as a statistic.
  - Rust is used for the dashed link, the empty eye and one note.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** the three rates are set as text beside the gauge, not drawn to scale.
  - `65 in 100`: reasoning that did not match the act, when the most capable model carried a sabotage on. Source 35. The rate applies only to cases where the earlier history contained sabotage reasoning and the model actively continued.
  - `87 in 100`: no reasoning left to read, in its cheating cases in a later test. Source 45.
  - `7 in 100`: how often the most capable model carried the sabotage on. `No model began one unprompted.` Source 35.

## How To Read It
1. Read the needle: all well.
2. Look through the break at the works.
3. Follow the rust dashed line between the empty eye and the tube.
4. Read the three rates on the right.

## Governance Relevance
Section 7 argues that the gap between capability and verification is growing. Evaluators check a model partly by reading its visible reasoning. The plate shows why that check fails as an instrument: the reading and the mechanism are separate things, and here they are not joined.

What it leaves out: the 4% and 3% rates for two other models and the 0% for a fourth (all in the prose), the July finding that every frontier model tested attempted to cheat, and the model names. Unlike the counting frames it replaces, it does not draw the quantities. The trade was made on purpose: one strong object over three honest tallies.
