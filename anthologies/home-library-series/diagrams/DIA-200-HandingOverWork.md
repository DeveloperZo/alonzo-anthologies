## What The Diagram Shows
The first picture of the 200 block, which looks inside one part: the hand-off. A job arrives at the Arbiter and goes one of three ways. Bulk reading with plain output goes to the quick route. Work that needs working out goes to the thinking route, which is the same home AI thinking for longer. Work that needs judgement stays with the Arbiter. Under the two home routes sit two safety catches, and under those the check the Arbiter makes on everything that comes back. A red band at the bottom states a rule: private things do not go this way yet.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** One entry box, three lanes, two safety catches, one check band, one rule band, one evidence note.
- **Relationships:** One arrow fans out to the three lanes. The two home lanes run down through a safety catch each and into the check band. The third lane ends: that work never leaves the Arbiter.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** Quick route (`local-fast`): 1 to 3 seconds for a short answer. Thinking route (`local-think`): about three times slower. "About 12,000 words" is the plain form of the 16,384 token window, question plus answer; it is an estimate. Scores from 12 kinds of job, each tried twice: quick route 18 right, 4 partly, 2 wrong; thinking route 19 right, 2 partly, 3 wrong. About 1 claim in 25 was wrong in summaries. Source: `client/ROUTING.md`, scored 2026-11-18. The rule band: the Arbiter is a paid AI today (Claude Code) and receives every answer the home AI writes, so a summary of a private document would reach that company. Until the Arbiter can be Zo's own model, private material goes through the chat page.

## How To Read It
1. Start at the top box and pick a lane by the grey words beside each arrow.
2. Read down a home lane through its safety catch.
3. Read the blue band: nothing from the home AI is used unchecked.
4. Read the red band: what must not be sent this way.
