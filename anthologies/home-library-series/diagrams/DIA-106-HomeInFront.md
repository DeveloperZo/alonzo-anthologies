## What The Diagram Shows
Drawn on 2026-11-18 from Zo's decisions that day: the home AI is always in front, version 1 of paid access is a plain switch, and paid AI is confined to one drive while private material lives on a drive it cannot read and is not told about.

You have two ways in. Private things go to the private door, a second gateway with only home routes. Everything else goes to the front door, which has a switch: home by default, paid if you flip it. Both doors lead to the home AI. Only the front door, switched to paid, reaches the paid AI.

There are two drives. The home AI reads both. A paid AI sees only the working drive.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** You; the private door (a second LiteLLM with no paid route); the front door (LiteLLM, with a paid pass-through route added); the home AI (Ollama); the paid AI (a pass-through route to a provider); the private drive; the working drive; a band of four rules.
- **Relationships:** Green lines never leave the house. Blue lines are the working side. Amber lines leave the house on purpose. The red bar shows that nothing runs from the paid AI to the private drive.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** The four rules are the hardening found on 2026-11-18. Rule 1 exists because a chat switched to paid part-way sends its whole history. Rule 2 is enforced by the hand-off tool, not by asking the model. Rule 3 exists because the logbook stores full text and a paid tool can query it. Rule 4 is Zo's: paid AI tools get drive F and no read access to drives C and D. Today that is a set of deny rules in Claude Code's settings, which stop its file tools but not a shell command; a separate Windows account is planned. What enforces each rule, and how strongly, is in `client/PRIVATE.md`. A second door is drawn because the front door today has one password and no database, so it cannot issue a home-only password. OneDrive runs on the desktop, hence "not synced to any cloud".
