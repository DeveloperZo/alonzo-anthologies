# DIA-101: The pieces
## Snapshot
- **Diagram file:** `_KNOWLEDGE/Diagrams/DIA-101-ThePieces.svg`
- **Generated by:** `_KNOWLEDGE/Diagrams/gen_site_100_101.py` (edit the generator, then run `python gen_site_100_101.py .` in that folder)
- **Theme:** Every piece of the publishing system, with a what, a why and a how-to-use
- **Primary audience takeaway:** There are nine pieces. Zo works in one of them, his computer. Everything after sending the changes up happens by itself.

## What The Diagram Shows
A table, because the content is a list. Each row gives a plain name with the real name in small print, what it is, why we have it, and how you use it. Rows are grouped: where the work is kept, the road to the site, what the public sees, never on the site. Two notes at the bottom record what is left over.

## Elements And Flow
- **Components:** Nine rows: Zo's computer, the online copy, the main copy, the publisher, the page maker, the live site, the resume app, a trial copy, the project notes. Two notes: the old duplicate site (`zo-garden`) and the old shelf, closed (GitLab, archived).
- **Relationships:** None drawn. DIA-100 shows the road the pieces form.
- **Notable labels or metrics:** Two addresses: `alonzo-anthologies.pages.dev` (the live site) and `/resume/` after it (the resume app). The page maker row says a file marked as a draft is left out. The trial copy row says a trial copy is public too.

## How To Read It
1. Read down the bold names: that is what the publishing system has.
2. Read across any row: what, why, how.
3. The group "what the public sees" is the part a visitor meets. The rest is behind the scenes.

## Architecture Relevance
The reference to keep open while reading DIA-100 and DIA-102 to DIA-105. The plain names here are the ones those pictures use.
